## Summary

This PNAS 2026 paper by Garcia Garcia et al. describes the engineering and decellularization of human hypertrophic cartilage (HyC) as an off-the-shelf bone graft substitute.

**Core concept:** They use the MSOD-B immortalized mesenchymal stem cell line to manufacture hypertrophic cartilage in vitro, then decellularize it (D-HyC) to create a purely extracellular matrix-based biomaterial that retains osteoinductive capacity while minimizing immunogenicity.

**Key findings:**
- Protocol 12 (sequential SDS 0.5%/12h + hypertonic/hypotonic buffers + DNase) achieved >99.7% decellularization with only 17.6% GAG loss, while preserving collagens and BMP-2
- D-HyC retains osteoinductivity in immunodeficient mice (full bone + marrow at 6 weeks ectopically), but fails ectopically in immunocompetent mice due to xenogeneic ECM rejection
- In vitro human allogeneic assays show D-HyC actively suppresses macrophage M1 polarization, blocks dendritic cell maturation, and fails to activate T cells — actual immunosuppression, not just low immunogenicity
- In immunocompetent rats, D-HyC achieved 13/13 critical femoral defect bridging at 12 weeks with 67% mechanical restoration

## Using D-HyC as an Artificial Skeleton for Osteoblast Experiments

This is actually quite well-suited for that purpose. A few approaches depending on what you want to measure:

**As a 3D scaffold for osteoblast culture:** The paper shows D-HyC powder primes primary hMSCs toward osteogenic differentiation (calcified matrix deposition) without affecting proliferation. You could grind D-HyC into powder and supplement osteoblast culture media, or use intact constructs as a 3D substrate for seeding. The preserved collagen network and BMP-2 provide the relevant cues.

**As an endochondral ossification template:** The more interesting use is implanting D-HyC as a template and studying how osteoblasts/osteoprogenitors colonize and remodel it. The paper's day 3–10 immune profiling data (Fig. 3) documents the early host cell invasion kinetics you'd need to account for in your experimental design.

**Practical limitations to keep in mind:**

- You can't directly replicate this without the MSOD-B cell line, which is a custom immortalized hMSC line (ref 12, Pigeot et al. 2021, *Advanced Materials*). That paper describes the manufacturing protocol and is the prerequisite read. The line may or may not be available from the authors — worth emailing Bourgine (paul.bourgine@med.lu.se).
- The decellularization protocol detail is in SI Appendix Fig. S1, not fully in the main text. You'd need the supplement.
- If you're working with primary hMSCs instead of MSOD-B, the paper's SI Appendix Fig. S2C/D gives you the powder supplementation assay as a validated starting point.
- The 17.6% GAG loss is worth noting if your experiments are GAG-sensitive (e.g., studying mechanotransduction).

What kind of osteoblast experiment are you designing? That would help narrow down whether you need the intact scaffold, powder supplementation, or just the conditioned matrix as a coating.
