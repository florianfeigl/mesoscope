\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Continuous, non-destructive observation of adherent cell cultures inside
perfused bioreactor chips currently requires either manual microscopy---which
removes the chip from the incubator and is operator-dependent---or expensive,
closed, single-vessel commercial live-cell imagers. This thesis designs, builds
and validates \emph{IDANA}, a mesoscope: an OpenFlexure-derived microscope on a
Raspberry~Pi~5 with a Hailo-8 neural-processing unit, tilted onto its side and
mounted on a linear rail so that a single imaging head scans a rack of
bioreactor chips in series and analyses cell-level images on-device, without
external compute infrastructure. The contribution spans three axes: a
reproducible port of the OpenFlexure Server~v3 stack to the Pi~5 with a
custom C-mount high-quality-camera optics module; a tilted, rail-mounted
imaging form factor with a Z-focus-only body and a parametric bioreactor rack;
and an edge-AI cell-detection pipeline trained on CVAT annotations and deployed
on the Hailo-8. We evaluate the system against optical, mechanical, automation,
machine-learning and cost criteria. \todo{final quantitative results once
experiments in milestones M1--M6 are complete.}
\end{abstract}
