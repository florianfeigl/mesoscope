\section{Electronics and Motion Control}
\label{sec:electronics}

\subsection{Compute stack}
The compute stack is a Raspberry~Pi~5 (\SI{8}{\giga\byte}) with the AI~HAT+
(Hailo-8, \SI{26}{TOPS}) on the PCIe interface and a DS3231 real-time clock on
the GPIO header for time-stamping unattended runs. The Hailo driver requires a
DKMS rebuild and a reboot in a specific order (Section~\ref{sec:software}).
The three boards stack inside the pull-out electronics drawer
(Fig.~\ref{fig:drawer}).

\subsection{Focus axis}
The Z-focus axis is driven by a 28BYJ-48 5\,V geared stepper through a ULN2003
driver board, as in the stock OpenFlexure Z actuator. Two further 28BYJ-48
motors from the original XYZ plan are on hand as spares.

\subsection{Rail axis}
The linear rail is driven by a NEMA17 stepper with an integrated Tr8$\times$2
leadscrew via a TMC2209 stepstick, with an anti-backlash nut, a coupler and an
end bearing. This axis carries the imaging head between chip stations.

\subsection{Stage driver architecture}
Motion is controlled from Python on the Pi~5 GPIO: the ULN2003 sequences the
focus stepper and the TMC2209 (step/dir) drives the rail. This replaces the
Sangaboard microcontroller used by stock OpenFlexure.
