\section{Optical Design}
\label{sec:optical}

\subsection{Imaging chain}
The imaging chain is: bioreactor chip $\rightarrow$ ZEISS Plan-Neofluar
$10\times/0.30$ objective $\rightarrow$ achromatic tube lens
($f = \SI{125}{\milli\meter}$, $\varnothing\,\SI{12.7}{\milli\meter}$)
$\rightarrow$ Raspberry~Pi HQ camera (Sony IMX477, C-mount). A
\SI{150}{\milli\meter} tube lens is held as a spare for a longer effective focal
length.

\subsection{Objective}
The objective is a ZEISS Plan-Neofluar $10\times/0.30$ (numerical aperture
NA~$=0.30$). Its diffraction-limited (Rayleigh) lateral resolution at
$\lambda = \SI{550}{\nano\meter}$ is
\begin{equation}
  d = \frac{0.61\,\lambda}{\mathrm{NA}}
    = \frac{0.61 \times \SI{550}{\nano\meter}}{0.30}
    \approx \SI{1.1}{\micro\meter}.
  \label{eq:rayleigh}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Sensor, magnification and sampling}
The IMX477 sensor is $6.287 \times \SI{4.712}{\milli\meter}$ with
\SI{1.55}{\micro\meter} pixels. With a nominal system magnification of
$\approx 0.515$ from the $10\times$ objective de-rated by the finite tube lens,
the object-space field of view and pixel sampling follow from the sensor
dimensions divided by the magnification. The resulting sampling comfortably
Nyquist-samples the $\approx\SI{1.1}{\micro\meter}$ optical resolution.

\subsection{C-mount optics module}
The tube lens is held in a printed C-mount module
(\texttt{optics\_hq\_125\_rms\_threaded.stl}) that mates the objective thread to
the HQ camera C-mount and sets the tube-lens-to-sensor spacing. The module and
its build script are documented in \texttt{hardware/optics/}.

\subsection{Illumination}
The stock OpenFlexure condenser arm cannot clear the \SI{16}{\milli\meter}-thick
bioreactor chip, so it is removed from the model and trans-illumination is
provided from behind the chip row (the $+Y$ side), passing through the
\SI{16}{\milli\meter} chip thickness to the objective on $-Y$
(\texttt{docs/RAIL\_MOUNT\_CONCEPT.md}, \S6.2). Rather than a fixed source per
station, a single collimated source is fibre-coupled and carried on the moving
imaging head, which keeps the hot, heavy LED assembly off the carriage and away
from the Pi~5 / Hailo-8 thermal budget; only the collimator tip travels behind
the chip currently in view.

The source is adapted from the mirror-based ModLight design~\cite{Gibson2023},
an open-hardware (CC~BY-SA-4.0) modular LED light source that delivers a
collimated beam through a light guide and has been demonstrated directly on
OpenFlexure. The mirror variant is chosen over the X-Cube-prism variant because
it reaches the near-infrared and because the step-and-image acquisition images
one channel at a time, so the prism's simultaneous wavelength mixing is not
needed. The target application---monitoring \emph{calcification} in the cultured
cells---drives the channel selection: calcium deposits stained with Alizarin
Red~S form a red complex absorbing around \SIrange{528}{560}{\nano\meter}, so a
green channel (\SIrange{525}{530}{\nano\meter}) maximises their brightfield
contrast; a white channel serves overview and von-Kossa-stained (dark) deposits;
and a near-infrared channel penetrates the thick, turbid chip with less
scattering and provides a label-free scattering signal from the birefringent
hydroxyapatite nodules. Switching the mirror between channels is manual
(magnet-held) in the published design, which is acceptable for per-scan channel
selection in the prototype; motorising it is a later option. \todo{specify the
final per-channel centre wavelengths, diffuser and irradiance once the
