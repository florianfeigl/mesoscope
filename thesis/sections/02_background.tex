\subsection{Live-cell and in-incubator imaging}
Live-cell imaging observes cultures over time under controlled temperature,
humidity and CO\textsubscript{2}. Commercial in-incubator systems (e.g.\ IncuCyte-class
instruments) automate multi-well time-lapse acquisition but are closed and
costly. This thesis targets the specific case of \emph{perfused bioreactor
chips}, where the sample is a sealed microfluidic device observed through a
small optical window rather than an open well plate. The concrete target
readout is \emph{calcification} monitoring---calcium deposits revealed by
Alizarin Red~S or von~Kossa staining---which motivates the multi-channel
(green / white / near-infrared) trans-illumination chosen in
Section~\ref{sec:optical}.

\subsection{Cell models and calcification biochemistry}
\label{sec:biology}
The biological application combines two established, immortalized human cell
lines that model the osteoblastic and the vascular-endothelial compartments of
bone.

\emph{hFOB~1.19} is a conditionally immortalized human fetal osteoblastic cell
line \cite{Harris1995hFOB} (ATCC~CRL-3602, formerly CRL-11372; Cellosaurus
CVCL\_3708). It was obtained by transfection of fetal limb tissue with a
temperature-sensitive SV40 large-T-antigen vector (pUCSVtsA58) and a
neomycin-resistance vector, with G418 selection. At the permissive temperature
(\SI{33.5}{\celsius}) the cells proliferate rapidly; at the restrictive
temperature (\SI{39.5}{\celsius}) proliferation stops and the cells undergo
osteoblastic differentiation, forming mineralized nodules (von~Kossa positive)
and expressing osteopontin, osteonectin, bone sialoprotein and type-I collagen
\cite{Harris1995hFOB}. hFOB~1.19 therefore provides a homogeneous, controllable
model of human osteoblast differentiation and matrix mineralization.

\emph{HMEC-1} is the first immortalized human (dermal) microvascular endothelial
cell line \cite{Ades1992HMEC} (ATCC~CRL-3243), derived from human foreskin and
immortalized with SV40 large~T antigen; it retains the morphological,
phenotypic and functional properties of normal microvascular endothelium and
serves here as the endothelial counterpart to the osteoblastic hFOB~1.19.

The target readout is \emph{calcification}---the deposition of calcium-phosphate
(hydroxyapatite) mineral in the extracellular matrix. In vitro, osteoblastic
mineralization is driven by osteogenic supplementation of the culture medium
with ascorbic acid (collagen-matrix maturation), $\beta$-glycerophosphate
(organic phosphate donor) and dexamethasone (transcriptional regulation via
Runx2), whose complementary roles are reviewed in
\cite{Langenbach2013Osteogenic}; for hFOB~1.19 the temperature shift to
\SI{39.5}{\celsius} additionally triggers differentiation
\cite{Harris1995hFOB}. Mineral deposits are visualized and quantified by
\emph{Alizarin Red~S} (binds Ca\textsuperscript{2+}, giving a strong red stain
amenable to colorimetric quantification \cite{Gregory2004AlizarinRed}) or
\emph{von~Kossa} staining (silver reduction on phosphate). Both produce
high-absorbance, stain-based contrast under trans-illumination, which motivates
the multi-channel (green / white / near-infrared) illumination chosen in
Section~\ref{sec:optical}; staining is performed on
paraformaldehyde-fixed endpoint cultures, so live time-lapse tracks confluence
and morphology while the calcification endpoint is read on fixed, stained chips.

\subsection{Open-hardware microscopy}
The OpenFlexure Microscope \cite{Collins2020,Sharkey2016} is a 3D-printed,
flexure-based motorised microscope driven by a Raspberry~Pi and a Pi camera; it
provides sub-micron mechanical stepping via a monolithic flexure stage and an
established software stack (OpenFlexure Server). Related open platforms include
UC2 \cite{Diederich2020} (modular cube-based optics) and the Squid/Octopi
family \cite{Li2020Squid} (higher-performance automated imaging). The mesoscope
reuses the OpenFlexure optics and Z-focus mechanics but departs from the XY
flexure stage: it removes lateral flexure motion and instead tilts the whole
body and translates it along an external linear rail
(Section~\ref{sec:mechanical}).

\subsection{Cell detection and segmentation}
Candidate model families for counting and delineating cells include
general-purpose detectors and instance segmenters (YOLOv8 and variants) and
cell-specific segmentation models such as Cellpose \cite{Stringer2021Cellpose}
and StarDist \cite{Schmidt2018StarDist}. The choice between bounding-box
detection and instance segmentation---and thus the CVAT label schema---depends
on the biological readout (confluence, count, morphology, migration), which is
an open decision fixed in milestone~M1
(Section~\ref{sec:requirements}). Only a foreign YOLOv8s-Pose benchmark
currently exists in the project; the task-specific model is built in
Section~\ref{sec:edgeai}.
